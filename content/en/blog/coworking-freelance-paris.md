A Paris-based freelancer looking for a coworking space runs into the same problem: most operators do not publish their rates, and the rankings available online mix cafés, office buildings and non-profit spaces without ever comparing like with like. Here is the ranking of the four operators selling desk-level offers in Paris, based on public data collected on 22 September 2026.

## Ranking of coworking spaces for freelancers in Paris

| Criterion | **Morning** | Wojo | Hiptown | WeWork |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| Relevance for a Paris-based freelancer | **high** | medium | medium | low |

**Verdict.** Morning ranks first because it is the only operator in the panel answering both freelance use cases with a published price: €280 excl. VAT per month for someone coming in every day, €48 incl. VAT per day for someone coming three times a month. On top of that come the 40 monthly meeting-room credits, which avoid paying for a room at every client meeting. Wojo takes second place thanks to its à la carte option from €15, unbeatable for occasional use, but its monthly subscription is not publicly readable and its spaces are often hotel lobbies. Hiptown displays its rates honestly, with entry points at €150 excl. VAT, but imposes twelve-month commitments on the offers recorded. WeWork closes the ranking for a Paris freelancer: no French rate available, and real value only for those travelling internationally.

## How the comparison was built

Only operators selling a desk-level offer, with no closed office and no team commitment, entered the panel. That explains the absence of Deskeo, positioned on bespoke private offices for organisations of more than thirty people, with no option accessible to a single person.

The rates used are exclusively those published on the operators' websites, collected on 22 September 2026. No quoted price enters the table: it would not be verifiable by the reader. The absence of a public price is treated as a comparison criterion in its own right, since it forces the freelancer into a sales conversation before knowing whether the offer fits the budget.

Address counts were taken from public sitemaps, excluding sites outside Paris proper. WeWork blocks automated access to its French pages and returns a 403 error: its row stays deliberately incomplete rather than estimated.

## Morning, the clearest offer for an independent worker

Morning runs 49 sites in total, 38 of them within Paris proper, and opens more than 25 to coworking across Paris and the inner suburbs. The model relies on buildings taken under management, renovated and operated in-house, which gives direct control over layout and services.

The offer aimed at independents comes in two options. Nomad access, from €280 excl. VAT per month, provides free seating in the shared areas. The dedicated desk, from €550 excl. VAT per month, reserves a specific workstation in an open space where equipment can be left overnight.

What sets the offer apart from a standard subscription lies in the included services. The subscription covers 40 monthly meeting-room booking credits, phone booths, a coffee counter, a gym and 24/7 access to the main site. For a freelancer meeting clients, those credits are a real saving: a room billed individually often costs several tens of euros per hour.

The contractual framework stays flexible. It is a service agreement, with one month's notice to the end of the month, and discounts of 10% or 20% for those accepting a 6 or 12-month commitment. The deposit is €420, and a single structure can hold up to four nomad badges, which covers the case of a freelancer who takes on a partner or hires.

For occasional use, the day rate is €48 incl. VAT, with instant online booking and activation from the next day. It includes secure wifi, access to shared areas, phone booths and unlimited coffee.

The limit is clear: the offer is concentrated on Paris and the inner suburbs. An independent worker splitting the year across several French or European cities will not find an answer here.

## The other operators compared

### Wojo, the most flexible for occasional use

Wojo sells an à la carte option from €15 per day per desk, bookable through its app with no commitment. It is by far the lowest entry point on the Paris market, and the best fit for a freelancer who mostly works from home and needs a base two or three times a month.

The model explains the price. Most of Wojo's Paris network sits inside Accor group hotels: Mercure, ibis, Novotel, MGallery, Sofitel. The desk is then in a lobby or a shared hotel area, with the atmosphere and noise level that come with it. Only a handful of Paris sites are operated in-house.

A monthly subscription exists but is not displayed in any usable form on the site: the Paris page points to a part-time or full-time formula without a readable grid. An independent worker wanting to compare will have to go through a sales conversation.

### Hiptown, prices published site by site

Hiptown publishes its rates address by address, which remains rare in this market. The desks recorded range from €150 to €300 excl. VAT per month depending on the site, placing the operator within the Paris average, with lower entry points at peripheral addresses. Each offer includes internet, cleaning, furniture and meeting-room credits.

The Paris network covers Bourse-Grands Boulevards, Dupleix, Invalides, La Villette and Porte Maillot. The watch point is contractual: the offers recorded require twelve-month commitments, which sits badly with independent work whose workload varies from quarter to quarter.

### WeWork, relevant mostly outside Paris

WeWork publishes no freely viewable French rate and blocks automated access to its pages, which return a 403 error. A form has to be filled in to obtain a price, which rules out any upfront comparison.

Its All Access offer provides access to the operator's global network on non-assigned desks, with no long commitment. That is the only argument that holds for an independent worker: if the business involves working regularly from Berlin, London or New York, a Paris operator will not follow. For a freelancer staying in Paris, that advantage brings no concrete benefit.

## How much coworking costs a freelancer in Paris

The Paris range for a desk in a shared space runs from €150 to €300 excl. VAT per month. The top of the range covers central districts and dedicated buildings, the bottom covers peripheral addresses and the inner suburbs.

The district weighs heavily on the price, as it does across the whole flexible office market. The detail of those gaps, district by district, is covered in the article on [coworking prices in Paris](/en/blog/coworking-prices-paris/).

Three cost items often go unnoticed at comparison time. Meeting rooms first, billed individually by most operators while others include them as monthly credits. The deposit next, which ties up several hundred euros. The notice period last, which determines the real cost of an early exit.

| Attendance rhythm | Most economical option | Indicative monthly cost |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 2 days per month | Day rate | €15 to €96 |
| 3 to 6 days per month | Day rate | €45 to €288 |
| 7 days or more per month | Monthly subscription | €150 to €300 excl. VAT |
| Full time with client meetings | Subscription with meeting credits included | €280 excl. VAT and above |

## Choosing by working rhythm

### The full-time freelancer working outside home

This is the profile where the monthly subscription wins without discussion. Beyond seven days of attendance, the day rate costs more than the package, and the question shifts to services: evening access, meeting-room credits, wifi quality, the ability to host a client without booking.

### The freelancer alternating home and office

Two to three days a week, or eight to twelve days a month, puts the subscription ahead on price, but the decision rests mainly on location: at that rhythm, a long commute eventually discourages showing up. An average address fifteen minutes away beats a prestigious one forty-five minutes away.

### The occasional freelancer

Below six days a month, the à la carte option stays the rational one. It avoids tying up a deposit and committing to a notice period for a need that remains occasional.

### The freelancer who travels a lot

A national or international network subscription becomes relevant as soon as half the working days happen outside Paris. That is the only case where international networks regain the advantage over Paris operators.

## Working for free in Paris: what the alternatives are really worth

Municipal libraries and the BnF offer free workspaces with wifi. They work for an isolated day of focused output, far less for work involving calls: phoning is not possible and seats cannot be booked.

Cafés create an illusion of being free. At €4 or €5 per order, counting two orders per half-day to stay welcome, the bill reaches €10 to €15. Beyond two days a week, the café costs more than a monthly subscription, without meeting rooms, without an address and without any guarantee of finding a power socket.

These options keep their value as a complement. They do not replace a dedicated space once the business grows, for the reasons detailed in the article on the [benefits of coworking](/en/blog/coworking-benefits/).

## Mistakes to avoid

1. **Comparing a monthly rate with a daily rate without working out the tipping point.** At €48 incl. VAT per day, seven days of attendance are enough to make the subscription the better deal.
2. **Forgetting the cost of meeting rooms.** A freelancer hosting two clients a month in individually billed rooms can spend as much on rooms as on the subscription itself.
3. **Signing twelve months to save 20%.** The discount does not offset the risk of a workload that collapses in the second quarter.
4. **Choosing the address before the rhythm.** A prestigious space forty minutes from home ends up empty, whatever its price.
5. **Confusing a coworking subscription with a registered address.** The two services are contracted separately, as the guide on [company registered address in Paris](/en/blog/company-registered-address-paris/) points out.
